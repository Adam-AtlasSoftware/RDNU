# RDNU baseline replacement: candidate review (2026-09-24)

Produced 2026-09-24 by a multi-agent literature/repo sweep (8 search angles, 22 candidate cards,
independent verification of 10, three rankers, critic and gap-fill pass), then reviewed and edited
by hand. Every table row carries its source. Numbers marked "est." or "inferred" were computed from
published layer lists or timings, not measured on RDNA hardware. Items marked "community, unverified"
are reverse-engineering write-ups that were not independently checked.

## 1. TL;DR

- **Top pick: Arm Neural Super Sampling v1.0.1 + Neural Graphics Model Gym.** Only candidate with released generalist weights (fp32 + INT8-QAT, three tiers), Apache-2.0 train/fine-tune/QAT code, engine-matched inputs (jittered LR colour, depth, MVs, history, recurrent feedback) and a counted cost of ~2.8K MAC/output px at 2x: 27x below the 36-channel RDG at the same scale (~76K) and 5.6x below the released 16-channel RDG-s at 2x (~16K). Ops are 3x3 conv / ReLU / sigmoid / nearest-up / concat only. Ranked #1 by two of three rankers (8.5, 9) and runner-up (7) by the third, whose top pick was STSSNet (7.5). Weights licence (Arm AI Model Community v1.0) verified verbatim: royalty-free, commercial, sublicensable, no Arm-hardware restriction; pass-through obligations only. Independently verified after the automated pass: files, licence text, single-GPU trainer, inputs, dataset, recipe defaults, and the MAC/parameter counts recomputed from source to the exact number (11,304 MAC per input pixel; 2,826 per output pixel at 2x; 684 for mid/low; 148,456 params).
- **Runner-up: STSSNet (AAAI 2024).** MIT, fully verified, ~7.6K MAC/px at 2x (paper 31.5 GFLOPs), gated-conv U-Net with a local kernel-prediction attention module, ingests the full G-buffer, and is the only open model that already does joint 2x SS + frame extrapolation. Weights are per-scene overfits, so treat it as "cheap to retrain" rather than "pre-trained".
- **RDG:** keep as the reference/ablation baseline and for its Cycles dataset and loss design; stop extending it. Computed from `rdg_arch.py`/`rdg_block.py`, the released RDG-s (16 ch, x4) costs only ~4.3K MAC/output px and the 36-ch RDG ~19.6K, both inside budget; the brief's "73K at 2x" is the 36-ch config at a hypothetical 2x (~76K computed). Replace it for structural reasons (direct-colour head that Arm and Sony both found quantises badly, no jitter-accumulation data, no path to RR/FG), not for cost.
- **Transformer vs Mamba:** no real-time transformer upscaler with open weights exists; DLSS 4's is 2.0-2.7x the CNN's frame time on NVIDIA's own table and NVIDIA-only. Mamba/SSM is blocked: sequential scan, no non-CUDA fast path, and NTIRE 2025/2026 Mamba entries were 6-20x slower than CNN winners at equal PSNR.
- **Budget correction:** RDNA2 is not 3-4x slower than RDNA3 for INT8 (RX 6800 XT ~83 TOPS DP4a vs RX 7900 XTX 123 TOPS WMMA; ComputerBase measured FSR4-INT8 costing the same on 6800 XT and 7800 XT). The cliff is RDNA3 -> RDNA4 (389 TOPS). Realistic 4K/2 ms target on a 7900 XTX is ~3-6K MAC/px, not 5-20K.
- **Production corroboration:** FSR4 (~4.5K MAC/px, ~116K params, verified from the leak) and Sony's PSSR 2.0 (SIGGRAPH 2026: launch colour-predicting U-Net replaced by an INT8 kernel-predicting CNN with ~90% of parameters at <=540p, training cut from ~4 GPU-months to under a GPU-week) are the two shipped designs at this budget; both are closed. AMD's stated position (CES 2026) is that FSR4 will be open-sourced "long-term" minus its core; SDK 2.3 (June 2026) is still DLL-only. Use both only as clean-room design references.

## 2. The budget reality

**Affordable MACs per OUTPUT pixel (network only), thousands.** Assumptions: 1 MAC = 2 ops; dense INT8 peak at boost clock; **40% sustained utilisation** (the figure Arm's NSS paper also assumes and vkpeak roughly measures on RDNA4); 100% of the budget inside the network (your own warp/blend/pack passes cost another 0.3-1.4 ms in FSR2/NSS practice); output pixel counts 1080p 2.07M, 1440p 3.69M, 4K 8.29M. Formula: MAC/px = t_s x TOPS x 1e12 x 0.4 / 2 / out_px.

Hard ceiling at 100% of peak is 2.5x these numbers (e.g. 7900 XTX 2 ms 4K = 14.8K). Three facts behind the table:

- **RDNA3 INT8 WMMA = FP16 WMMA rate.** `V_WMMA_I32_16X16X16_IU8` is 512 ops/clk/CU, the same as F16; only IU4 doubles (246 TOPS). The ISA says WMMA "internally use the DOT instructions" (https://web.archive.org/web/2023id_/https://developer.amd.com/wp-content/resources/RDNA3_Shader_ISA_December2022.pdf; https://gpuopen.com/learn/wmma_on_rdna3/). RDNA2 DP4a is also 512 ops/clk/CU, so WMMA on RDNA3 buys issue-slot/VGPR efficiency, not MACs.
- **Measured product cross-check.** FSR4 INT8 (leaked build) at 1440p cost +1.66 ms on a 7800 XT and +2.17 ms on a 6800 XT over FSR 3.1; ComputerBase: "RDNA 2 ist nicht langsamer als RDNA 3" (https://www.computerbase.de/artikel/grafikkarten/fsr-4-rdna-2-rdna-3.94512/seite-2). Official FSR 4.1 INT8 on a 7900 XTX at 4K Performance: +2.2 ms over FSR 3.1 (https://www.computerbase.de/artikel/grafikkarten/fsr-upscaling-4-1-rdna-3-test.98045/seite-3).
- **Small-channel nets do not reach 40%.** AMD's official FSR4 times (4K: 1.316 ms on 9070 XT, 3.07 ms on 7900 XTX, https://gpuopen.com/amd-fsr-upscaling/) against the verified 37 GMAC network give ~28 TMAC/s (~15% of 389 TOPS) and ~12 TMAC/s (~20% of 123 TOPS). For a 16-64-channel network, halve the table.

**What "transformer vs CNN vs Mamba" means at 3-6K MAC/px:**

- **CNN** is what ships. FSR4 is ~116K parameters of ConvNeXt/FasterNet blocks at 16/32/64 channels; NSS is 148K parameters of plain 3x3 convs; both predict filter kernels + blend weights rather than colour. Arm found a direct-colour U-Net "quantizes badly for HDR" (banding) while kernel/parameter prediction "quantizes well" (https://huggingface.co/Arm/neural-super-sampling/resolve/main/2025-neural-super-sampling.pdf). Sony's PSSR 2.0 deck (SIGGRAPH 2026, speaker notes) reports the same lesson from production: the launch colour-predicting network had to "store and reproduce HDR frame buffers in 8-bit registers", forced "most training to run quantized", produced colour fringing and took "~4 GPU-month" to train; the replacement kernel-predicting network "outputs blend weights rather than color", which "fit naturally in 8-bit", uses an explicit weighted-sum blend ("this gating structure is a GRU") that fixed gradient flow, applies a 3-parameter anisotropic-Gaussian kernel in *input* space so its reach scales with the upscale ratio, puts "~90% of parameters at 540p or below", and trained in "under a GPU week" (https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/content/siggraph2026_advances_dcraig_v1_0_publish.pdf). Output-head choice matters more than backbone family for INT8; RDG's PixelShuffle colour head is the wrong side of that line.
- **Transformer** is feasible but ~2-2.7x the CNN cost at equal task. NVIDIA's own guide: 4K Performance, CNN 0.53 ms vs transformer J/K 1.06 ms on a 4090; 1.30 vs 3.50 ms on a 2080 Ti (FP16-only, ~114 TFLOPS, i.e. roughly a 7900 XTX's class). "Double the parameters" is official (https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss4-multi-frame-generation-ai-innovations/). A community reverse-engineering write-up (unverified) describes the DLSS 4.5 SR net as ~1.26M params, 11 fused local-window stages on 8x8 output tiles with 10x10 padded neighbourhoods, 1x1 convs only, RMS-style norm, polynomial activation, base-2 clamped softmax, outputting 10 filter params/px for an anisotropic Gaussian over a cheap base upscale (https://gist.github.com/madebyollin/f87b506b2779c2aeae5e44c81ef0fdee). Every op is HLSL/WMMA-friendly; two community projects (unverified) claim windowed attention running from hand-written compute shaders (https://github.com/maanHimself/OpenDLSS-NR, https://github.com/lmxxf/dlss5-on-amd-9070xt-porting). But no open real-time transformer upscaler with weights exists; academic "light" SR transformers are 5-50x over budget. Sony explicitly stayed with "a highly optimized CNN variant" for PSSR 2.0 (https://playfront.de/en/sony-pssr-2-0-wechsel-auf-kernel-prediction-bringt-mehr-bildstabilitaet-auf-der-ps5-pro/).
- **Mamba/SSM** is blocked at this budget (section 5).

## 4. Detailed cards (top 5)

### 4.1 Arm NSS v1.0.1 + Model Gym (top pick)

**Architecture** (from `model_blocks_v1.py`, matched to `nss_v1_0_1_high_fp32.pt` tensor shapes). Pre-pass (Slang in the gym, GLSL in the HF `scenario/` folder): depth scatter/dilation, disocclusion mask (view-space depth test), YCoCg luma-derivative flicker signal, Catmull-Rom history reprojection, reprojection of the previous 4-ch feedback tensor (zeroed on disocclusion), packed into one 12-ch tensor at render res: [0-2] reprojected history, [3-5] current 1spp colour, [6] disocclusion, [7-10] feedback, [11] luma derivative. Network: E0 conv 12->32 s2, 32->32; E1 32->32 s2, 32->32; E2 32->64 s2, 64->64; bottleneck 64->32; D2 nearest-up, 32->16, cat skip (48)->32; head A 32->36 + sigmoid = 6x6 kernel per 4x4 input block (high; 4x4 kernel in mid/low); D1 up, 32->16, cat (48)->16, 16->16; head B 16->4 + sigmoid = theta (history rectification), alpha (accumulation), and the feedback for next frame. 148,456 params (high), 142,676 (mid/low). Post-pass: apply kernel to jittered LR colour, theta-rectify history, alpha-accumulate, Reinhard domain. No norm layers, no attention. The kernel is applied in input (LR) space, the same choice Sony made for PSSR 2.0 so that kernel reach scales with the upscale ratio.

**Ops:** 3x3 conv (stride 1/2, bias), ReLU, sigmoid, nearest 2x up, concat; shader passes for warps and KPN application. All map to DP4a or INT8 WMMA.

**Cost arithmetic.** Per input pixel N (high: 960x540): conv0 864 + conv1 2304 + conv2 576 + conv3 576 + conv4 288 + conv5 576 + conv6 288 + conv7 288 + conv8 864 + kpn 648 + conv9 1152 + conv10 1728 + conv11 576 + temporal 576 = 11,304 MAC -> 5.86 GMAC per 1080p frame = 11.7 GOPs (Arm's deck: "about 10 GOPs" for the 4x4-head variant). Per output pixel at 2x: **2,826 MAC**; 4K output = 23.4 GMAC. Inferred RDNA time at 20-40% of peak: 7900 XTX 4K 0.95-1.9 ms, 1080p 0.24-0.48 ms; 6800 XT (83 TOPS) 4K 1.4-2.8 ms; mid/low is 4x cheaper. No desktop measurement exists.

**Weights.** https://huggingface.co/Arm/neural-super-sampling/tree/main: `nss_v1_0_1_high_fp32.pt` (604 kB), `high_int8.pt/.vgf`, `mid_low_int8.pt/.vgf`; INT8 metadata: single input tensor scale 0.00391 zp -128, outputs scale 0.00394 zp -127 (symmetric, ExecuTorch TOSAQuantizer). Export your own INT8 tables from the QAT state dict; the VGF files are Vulkan-only.

(6) Non-2x scales are configurable but unproven; Sony reports 2.5x-3x needs the input-space kernel to keep reach, which NSS already has. ### 4.2 STSSNet (runner-up)

**Architecture** (verified from `model.py`). Lightweight gated-conv U-Net (LWGatedConv2D = conv + sigmoid mask; encoder ~24/24/32/32, decoder 96+32->32->56->24->48->24), a separate history feature stack (convHis1-3, 24->24->32) fused into the decoder, PixelShuffle(2) output with a bilinear-upsampled residual skip. Efficient Reshading Module (ERM, 155.5K of 417.2K params): unfold a 5x5 neighbourhood, similarity-weighted aggregation of warped history to inpaint disocclusion holes (trained with Random Reshading Masking). The same network alternates between producing a 2x supersampled frame and an extrapolated future frame. No DCN, no recurrence/scan (confirmed).

**Ops:** 3x3 gated conv, strided gated conv, bilinear up + concat, PixelShuffle, 5x5 unfold + weighted aggregation (local KPN/attention), bilinear residual.

**Cost arithmetic.** Paper: 31.502 GFLOPs, 417.241K params at 960x540 -> 1920x1080. 31.502e9 / 2,073,600 px = 15,193 FLOP/px -> **7,596 MAC/px** (1 MAC = 2 FLOP is the card's convention, not the paper's). Measured 4.35 ms on RTX 3090 (Torch-TensorRT FP16): backbone 3.39 + ERM 0.83 + history 0.12. Derived: 15.75 GMAC / 4.35 ms = 3.6 TMAC/s, ~5% of a 3090's FP16 tensor peak, i.e. the pipeline is memory/gather-bound, not MAC-bound; expect the HLSL port's time to be dominated by the unfold/gather and gating, not the convs. At 4K output (63 GMAC): 2.6-5.1 ms on a 7900 XTX at 40-20% (inferred), borderline; at 1080p/1440p output it fits.

**Weights.** `checkpoints/{Arena,Lewis,Subway,SunTemple}/finalModel.pkl`, one per training scene (confirmed present). Not a generalist model.

**Training on 4x3090.** `train.py`: 100 epochs single-scene / 50 cross-scene, Adam 1e-4, StepLR(50, 0.9), RandomCrop 256 (4 crops/image), batch 2, single `cuda:0`; PyTorch 1.11 / CUDA 11.4 / TensorRT 8.2.5. Dataset on ModelScope (https://www.modelscope.cn/datasets/ryanhe312/STSSNet-AAAI2024): ~6,000 train + ~1,000 test frames per scene, 16-bit numpy, ~40 GB/test scene (150 GB/scene train unverified). One 3090 per scene; four scenes in parallel.

**Risks.** Per-scene overfit weights; jitter pattern undocumented (must be reverse-engineered before matching RDNU's capture); dataset licence unstated (code MIT confirmed); FP16 only, no INT8 evidence, and it predicts colour directly (the head Arm/Sony found quantises worst); one size; ERM is a non-standard op with no HLSL prior art; single-paper repo with no maintenance signal.

**Links.** https://github.com/ryanhe312/STSSNet-AAAI2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10890; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ryanhe312/STSSNet-AAAI2024/main/model.py; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ryanhe312/STSSNet-AAAI2024/main/train.py.

### 4.3 AMD FSR4 "fsr4_model_v07" topology (clean-room reference only)

**Architecture** (verbatim shapes from the leaked ML2Code output, cross-checked against `uint4 logicalSize` entries in `passes_1080.hlsl`; both cards verified). Runs at OUTPUT resolution on a 7-ch tensor (4 reprojected recurrent channels + luma of upsampled current + luma of reprojected previous output + colourfulness). pass0 conv 2x2 s2 7->16 (960x540); pass1-2 ConvNeXt blocks (dw 3x3 16, pw 16->32, pw 32->16); pass3 conv 2x2 s2 16->32; pass4-5 FasterNet blocks (partial 3x3 on 16 of 32 ch, pw 32->64->32); pass6 conv 2x2 s2 32->64 (240x135); pass7-9 FasterNet64 (partial 3x3 16->32, pw 64->128->64); convT 2x2 64->32 + skip; pass10-11 FasterNet32; convT 32->16 + skip; pass12-13 ConvNeXt16; convT 16->8 to full res. ReLU (ReluClampS8), no norm. 8 outputs/px: oriented-Gaussian 3x3 kernel params (tilt = tanh, scales = 2/(1+e^-x)), sigmoid history-blend factor, 4 recurrent channels. Pre-pass: YCbCr, MV dilation by depth, disocclusion, 9-tap Catmull-Rom reprojection of history and recurrent texture; post: 3x3 kernel over jittered LR colour, blend, clamp [0, 64000], then RCAS and SPD auto-exposure. 14 model + 13 padding dispatches; ~20 MB scratch at 1080p. ~116K params. Note the contrast with its Project Amethyst sibling: FSR4 runs its U-Net at output resolution (first stride-2 conv brings it to 960x540), whereas Sony's PSSR 2.0 "does very little at 4K" and puts ~90% of parameters at <=540p; NSS sits at input resolution throughout. Both production designs share the 3-parameter anisotropic-Gaussian kernel + explicit blend + small recurrent state.

**Cost arithmetic** (verifier-recomputed). pass0 232 M + ConvNeXt16 x4 2,422 M + pass3 265 M + FasterNet32 x4 3,318 M + pass6 265 M + FasterNet64 x3 2,040 M + transposed convs 796 M = 9,339.5 MMAC per 1080p frame -> **4,504 MAC/output px** (6,664 if conv_dw were dense). Independent of scale factor. AMD measured 4K (37 GMAC): 9070 XT 1.316 ms (FP8 WMMA) ~15% of peak; 7900 XTX 3.07 ms (INT8 dot4) ~20% of peak; 7800 XT 1440p 2.05 ms; 7600 1080p 2.0 ms (https://gpuopen.com/amd-fsr-upscaling/).

**Weights and open-source status (checked 2026-09).** Official: signed `amd_fidelityfx_upscaler_dx12.dll` only, "no reverse engineering". Leaked (Aug 2025, "posted on GPUOpen GitHub in error"): `initializers.bin` 89,216 B INT8 / 130,088 B FP8 per tier (six tiers), per-tensor scales baked into HLSL (e.g. 0.01488), FP32 accumulate; no ONNX/FP32 anywhere; generated files carry "All rights reserved" headers while the repo carried an MIT LICENSE file. At CES 2026 AMD's Andrej Zdravković said open-sourcing FSR 4 is "the long-term plan" and AMD wants to "work as openly as possible", but that source would be published while keeping "key aspects of the technology's core functionality" closed; no date (Tom's Hardware roundtable, 2026-01-07, https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/amd-fsr-redstone-press-roundtable-ces-2026; https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/graphics-cards/amd-suggests-that-fsr-4-will-eventually-be-open-sourced-bar-the-core-technology-as-part-of-its-long-term-plan/; https://en.gamegpu.com/news/zhelezo/amd-planiruet-otkryt-iskhodnyj-kod-fsr-4). As of FSR SDK 2.3 (June 2026) everything ML is still delivered as signed DLLs behind `amd_fidelityfx_loader.dll`; FSR Upscaling 4.1.1 is official on RX 9000 and RX 7000, while Frame Generation, Ray Regeneration and Radiance Caching are RX 9000+ only (https://gpuopen.com/amd-fsr-sdk/). Sony's PSSR 2.0 is "a custom PlayStation implementation built on that shared research and training" and runs INT8 ("FSR Upscaling uses 8-bit floating point, and PSSR uses 8-bit integer", Cerny), so the Amethyst network family demonstrably ships in INT8 (https://www.notebookcheck.net/PlayStation-5-s-PSSR-2-0-uses-AMD-s-FSR-4-s-INT8-version-that-may-skip-PC-RDNA-2-3-GPUs.1258631.0.html). Do not plan on a legally reusable model: even the promised release excludes the "core", which is the network/weights. Do not ship, do not fine-tune from.

**Training on 4x3090.** Nothing exists: reimplement the topology in PyTorch (~300 lines), reimplement pre/post as differentiable ops, build your own jittered-render dataset, invent the loss/BPTT/QAT recipe. GPU time is small (~9 GMAC/frame, 120K params: batch 2-4 x 8-32 frames at 1080p fits 24 GB; by the NSS arithmetic above, hours per run on a 50K-frame set); engineering time is weeks.

**Risks.** Legal taint even from reading the generated code; six per-preset weight sets suggest per-scale training; RDNA2 marginal even for AMD (RX 6750 XT leaked-DLL test: 10-15% uplift at 1440p Quality, https://www.techspot.com/article/3040-amd-fsr4-upscaling-older-radeon-gpu/; official RDNA2 slipped to early 2027); AGS intrinsics (`amd_mixed_dot`, `AmdWaveMatrix*`) need a `dot4add_i8packed`/plain wave-matrix port and lhl found scalar INT8 beating packed dot on gfx1151; the INT8 WMMA branch in the operators is untested.

### 4.4 RDG (incumbent; keep as reference)

### 4.5 SMFANet (spatial-block donor; rank 5 by two of three rankers)

## 5. Mamba / SSM verdict

**Verdict: blocked for RDNU's runtime, and not competitive at this budget even on CUDA.** Evidence:

1. **Runtime-scored challenge data.** NTIRE ESR (RTX A6000, FLOPs at 256x256 LR x4, PSNR floor ~26.9 dB): 2025 Mamba entry GXZY_AI 101.0 ms / 25.88G, rank 34 vs winner EMSR 9.99 ms / 8.54G; 2026 MambaGate-SR 38.67 ms / 33.40G rank 11 and DWMamba 108.7 ms / 19.34G rank 14 vs SPANV2 5.26 ms / 9.11G. At equal PSNR the SSM entries needed 2-3.7x the FLOPs and 6-20x the wall time; the 2026 report says Mamba's "practical advantage in runtime-oriented challenges remains limited" (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10686; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03198v1).
2. **Independent latency table.** MambaIR 2.599 s, MambaIRv2 1.918 s, EVSSM 1.267 s vs SwinIR 0.823 s, Restormer 0.853 s; attributed to immature fused kernels, permutations from scan reordering and GPU synchronisation (https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01760; conditions not stated).
3. **No portable fast path.** Mamba-1's selective scan exists only as custom CUDA (state-spaces/mamba, VMamba's selective_scan + Triton cross-scan, 2DMamba). Pure-PyTorch scans are ~2x slower and memory-bound once state is materialised (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alxndrTL/mamba.py/main/README.md). ggml ships SSM_SCAN Vulkan/WebGPU/Metal shaders, but each is a serial loop over tokens with parallelism only over heads x state x batch, fine for LLM decode, useless for a 2M-token image at 1-3 ms; Vulkan had no SSM kernel at all until 2026 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/ops.md; https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/src/ggml-vulkan/vulkan-shaders/ssm_scan.comp). mamba-metal shows an associative affine scan in MSL (wave prefix-scan style, portable to SM6 WavePrefix*) but requires FP32 state and publishes no timings (https://github.com/SNAPKITTYWEST/mamba-metal). SF-Mamba (2026) still concedes "relatively slow computational speed with short token lengths typical in visual tasks" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16423). ViM-Q needed an FPGA pipeline to make the recurrence fast at low batch (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01935).
4. **No INT8 path for the scan.** PTQ4VM leaves the selective scan unquantised because it is register/shared-memory bound (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20386); QMamba's 8-bit hidden state costs ~4 points on Vim-S (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13624); Q-MambaIR's Jetson INT8 gains come from operator fusion, not low-bit scan math (https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21970). DP4a/WMMA only ever accelerate the projections around the scan.
5. **When SSMs pay off.** MambaOut: only for long-sequence AND autoregressive tasks; gated-CNN blocks with the SSM removed beat every vision Mamba on ImageNet (https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07992). A real-time upscaler's only autoregressive axis is time, one step per frame, where an SSM degenerates to a gated exponential moving average, which is what FSR4/NSS/DLSS already implement as a learned blend factor over a recurrent state, and what Sony describes PSSR 2.0's explicit blend as ("this gating structure is a GRU").
6. **The one escape hatch.** Mamba-2/SSD's chunked form is 3/4 batched matmuls (WMMA-portable) with a short inter-chunk pass (https://tridao.me/blog/2024/mamba2-part3-algorithm/; Metal proof in https://github.com/sg-systems-co/helix). With chunk = spatial window and no cross-chunk pass it equals decay-masked windowed linear attention. No vision-SR model uses it. If you ever test an SSM, test that, at the coarsest U-Net level only (MobileMamba pattern, https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15941).

## 6. Path to a Ray Reconstruction analogue and to frame generation

### 6.1 Joint denoise + upscale (RR analogue)

- **Vendor state of play.** AMD FSR Ray Regeneration 1.2.0 is a *decoupled* ML denoiser run before upscaling, RDNA4-only, binary-only; card-reported cost 1.13 ms at 960x540, 4.09 ms at 1080p, 7.72 ms at 1440p on a 9070 XT for four signals (timings unverified) (https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/denoising/). Inputs: noisy direct/indirect diffuse and specular, AO, dominant-light visibility, specular occlusion, depth, MVs, octahedral normal+roughness, sqrt-albedo. Arm's NSSD is announced (compute/neural/compute sandwich, multi-res pyramid, temporal feedback) with no weights or code as of 2026-09 (https://developer.arm.com/mobile-graphics-and-gaming/neural-technology). Intel OIDN 3 (temporal, kernel prediction, "supersampling") is announced for H2 2026, unreleased (https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/01/open-image-denoise-3-will-support-temporal-denoising/).
- **What the budget says.** Even OIDN FAST (62K MAC/px, native res) measures 6.03 ms at 1080p on a 7900 XTX and 38 ms on a 6800 XT (https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000195383). An RR-class network at render res must be an FSR4/NSS-sized net (5-10K MAC per *render* pixel) with a kernel-prediction head, plus demodulation.
- **Reusable pieces.** (a) NSRD's radiance demodulation: upscale/denoise the smooth lighting term, re-modulate with HR albedo; conv-only, MIT, and the paper reports the module at 0.26 GFLOPs (https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06699v2). (b) OIDN as Apache-2.0 teacher/initialiser and as a training-toolkit template (L1+MS-SSIM, DDP, .tza export) (https://github.com/RenderKit/oidn-weights). (c) FSR4/NSS/PSSR-2-style KPN + explicit blend + recurrent-state design, which Arm and Sony both report quantises well under INT8 while colour-predicting U-Nets band or fringe (https://huggingface.co/Arm/neural-super-sampling/resolve/main/2025-neural-super-sampling.pdf; https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/content/siggraph2026_advances_dcraig_v1_0_publish.pdf). (d) AMD FidelityFX Denoiser 1.1.4 (MIT, HLSL) for hand-written reprojection/variance/disocclusion passes around a small net (https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-denoiser/). (e) denox's fused Vulkan conv/upsample/concat kernels as a reference for HLSL U-Net dispatch fusion (https://github.com/kistenklaus/denox).
- **Data.** Noisebase SampleSet v1 (Apache-2.0 code, MIT per paper): 1,024 x 64-frame sequences of 256x256 patches, 32 spp per-sample radiance, normals, albedo, MVs, depth, 6144-spp references, 1.84 TB train (https://balint.io/noisebase/datasets/sampleset_v1.html). Gaps: no separated diffuse/specular, no roughness/hit-distance, no full frames. BMFR (7 scenes x 60 frames, 1 spp + features, ~19 GB) if the tuni.fi link is revived (https://github.com/maZZZu/bmfr). For DLSS-RR-style inputs you will render your own with Falcor (BSD; Noisebase's generator is Falcor-based) (https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Falcor). Co-design the sample pattern with the filter footprint (FAST, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15364). Evaluate with CGVQM+D (Apache-2.0, built for neural denoiser/upscaler artefacts) (https://github.com/IntelLabs/CGVQM).

### 6.2 Frame generation

- **Already-open, retrainable:** Arm NFRU v1 (verified card): interpolation T-1/T+1 -> T; four candidates (T-1/T+1 warped by engine MVs and by block-matching optical flow) + warped depths + double-disocclusion mask -> 104K-param INT8 CNN at 270x480 -> 4 softmax blend weights at 1080p; ~6.3 GMAC per generated frame, ~3.0K MAC/px at 1080p (counted), colour never passes through the network. Port cost: replace `VK_ARM_data_graph_optical_flow` with the Apache-2.0 GLSL/PyTorch BlockMatch v3.2.1 references (https://github.com/arm/ai-ml-emulation-layer-for-vulkan/tree/main/graph/shaders/optical_flow; https://github.com/arm/neural-graphics-model-gym/blob/main/src/ng_model_gym/usecases/nfru/model/optical_flow/blockmatch_v321.py) or FSR 3.1's MIT HLSL optical flow and retrain; reuse FSR3's frame-interpolation swapchain for pacing. Half-frame latency, linear-motion assumption, no extrapolation variant.
- **Extrapolation (zero added latency):** STSSNet already does joint SS + extrapolation (~75% faster than an ExtraNet+NSRR two-stage pipeline per the paper). ExtraNet is the reference design (occlusion motion vectors, gated-conv fill) but has no licence, one scene, ~50K MAC/px and an unbuildable UE 4.25 data pipeline (https://github.com/fuxihao66/ExtraNet); GFFE/ExtraSS/PatchEX supersede it without code (https://poiw.github.io/ExtraSS/).
- **Non-ML fallback already in hand:** FSR3 frame interpolation, optical flow and swapchain are MIT HLSL in the FidelityFX SDK (https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK); Arm's NFRU runtime is literally a FidelityFX-1.1.3 derivative, so the two compose. AMD's ML Frame Generation 4.0.1 is RDNA4-only, DLL-only (2.2 ms at 4K on a 9070 XT, card-reported) (https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/frame-interpolation-ml/).
- **Integration contract:** match NVIDIA Streamline's DLSS-G input spec (depth, MVs, HUD-less colour, UI alpha, camera constants) so OptiScaler-style bridges can inject RDNU FG into shipped games (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Streamline/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS_G.md; https://github.com/optiscaler/OptiScaler).
- **Free speed-up for any U-Net:** ReFrame layer caching, 1.4x average (up to 1.85x) by persisting deep features across frames (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13814).

### 7.2 Recipe (network: NSS-high as the vehicle; same recipe applies to an RDNA3-tier widened variant)

1. **Week 0 sanity (1 GPU, hours):** `pip install ng-model-gym`, download the Bistro set, `ng-model-gym evaluate` the released fp32 and INT8 checkpoints, then `train --finetune` for a few epochs to confirm the pipeline end to end (~10-15 min for a 5K-frame fine-tune by the arithmetic in 4.1).
2. **Data (the real cost, ~2-3 weeks of engineering):** capture 50K+ frames across diverse scenes in Arm's EXR/safetensors layout (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arm/neural-graphics-model-gym/main/docs/nss/nss_dataset_specification.md): jittered linear-HDR LR colour R16G16B16A16F, forward-Z depth R32F, non-jittered UV-space MVs at LR, jitter in pixels, FovX/FovY, near/far, ViewProjection, exposure, CameraCut; GT = 8K render box-filtered 4x4 (16 spp), i.e. the Facebook NSRR convention (https://research.facebook.com/publications/neural-supersampling-for-real-time-rendering/). If RDNU's engine is not Unreal, write the exporter to that spec; Blender Cycles can emit Vector/Z/Normal/Diffuse Color passes but jitter must be scripted (https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/layers/passes.html).
3. **fp32 training:** default v1 config (batch 8, 16-frame recurrent unroll, 15 epochs, AdamW 1e-3, cosine, 5% warm-up, 256-px crops, L1 + 0.157 LPIPS + 0.7 flicker, 30% GT-history seeding, `train.compile: true`). Memory: Arm's batch-16 x 16-frame setting needs ~40 GB, so on 24 GB use batch 8 x 16 or batch 16 x 8. Expected wall time: **~1-2 h per fp32+QAT run on 50K frames** (computed in 4.1: ~1 TFLOP/step dominated by the LPIPS loss, ~5,900 steps, 0.3-1 s/step), which is the same order as the Facebook NSRR analogue (~1.5 days on a Titan V for a far larger network) and two orders below Sony's "under a GPU week" for a production-scale KPN on a ~100-title corpus. No DDP in the gym: run four independent experiments (width x1/x2 for the RDNA3 tier, 1.5x/2x/3x scale, KPN 4x4 vs 6x6) on the four cards; at these run lengths you can afford dozens of variants per week, so data quality, not GPU time, gates progress.
4. **Quantisation-aware training:** 2 QAT epochs at 1e-4 with the gym's ExecuTorch TOSAQuantizer profile (symmetric per-tensor INT8 weights and activations; sigmoid heads keep outputs in [0,1]). Keep fp32 for the bulk of training and QAT only as a tail: Sony attributes most of its 4-GPU-month -> 1-GPU-week reduction to no longer having to train quantised, which the KPN head makes possible. Export the QAT state dict and scales to RDNU's own INT8 tables; mirror FSR4's runtime convention (INT8 operands, INT32/FP32 accumulate, requantise once per layer output, `dot4add_i8packed` on RDNA2, wave-matrix on RDNA3). Keep the pre/post passes and the KPN application in FP16.
5. **Validation:** PSNR/SSIM/STLPIPS as in the gym, plus FLIP, tPSNR and CGVQM against RDG on the same held-out sequences; log ms at 1080p/1440p/4K on a 6800 XT and a 7900 XTX with RGP.
6. **Second track (GPU 4):** reproduce STSSNet on one scene (100 epochs) to have an open joint SS+FG reference and to measure what its unfold/ERM costs in HLSL.

## 9. Risks and the first one-week experiment

**Risks of the pick.**
1. Quality evidence is thin: Arm's only quantitative comparison is against Arm ASR (FSR2-derived), and no RDNA or desktop timing exists. The MAC count, licence and trainer claims are verified; the quality ceiling is not.
2. Weights/dataset licence is not OSI: AUP pass-through to end users, licence copy and notices shipped, modifications marked, no reverse-engineering of the model (verified terms in 4.1). Mitigation: retrain from scratch on your own captures with the Apache-2.0 code; only Arm-derived weights carry the licence. Have counsel confirm the 4(d) reading.
3. Quality ceiling: block-shared 6x6 kernels, 4-ch state, 148K params, only shown to beat an FSR2 derivative. Mitigation: the budget allows 2-4x widening on RDNA3; the design is the same family as FSR4, PSSR 2.0 and DLSS2, and Sony's data says put the added capacity at <=540p.
4. No desktop or RDNA timing exists; Vulkan-shaped runtime. Mitigation: experiment below.
5. Data: Bistro sample is small; production data is your job (Arm: >50K frames for the released model, "substantially larger" for production). UE-only capture plugin.
6. Scale factors other than 2x unproven; RDNA2 Performance/Ultra-Performance tiers need retraining. Sony found 2.5x more common than expected and needed the input-space kernel for it; NSS has that property, so a 2.5x/3x retrain is a config change, not a redesign.

**Week-one experiment (go/no-go for the pick).**
- Day 1: install the gym, evaluate `nss_v1_0_1_high_fp32.pt` and the INT8 checkpoint on the Bistro test split; record PSNR/FLIP; run a 1-epoch `--finetune` to prove the loop and to measure the real s/step against the 0.3-1 s estimate in 4.1.
- Day 2: dump the INT8 state dict and scales; generate a golden set of pre-pass tensors and post-pass outputs for 8 frames with the gym's pure-torch backend.
- Days 2-4: write the 14 conv layers in HLSL on RDNU's existing INT8 kernels (3x3 stride 1/2, ReLU, nearest-up, concat, sigmoid), port pre/post from Slang (or the HF GLSL) to HLSL compute, apply the quarter-res KPN grid through texture upsampling as Arm does.
- Day 5: bit-compare against the golden tensors (tolerance = one INT8 step per layer), then time at 1080p and 4K on a 6800 XT and a 7900 XTX with RGP. Pass criteria: 4K <= 2 ms network + <= 1 ms pre/post on the 7900 XTX; <= 4 ms on the 6800 XT (inferred targets: 0.95-1.9 ms and 1.4-2.8 ms).
- Days 6-7: capture two RDNU sequences with jitter/depth/MV in Arm's spec, run NSS vs RDG-s on them, score FLIP/tPSNR/CGVQM. Decide: if timing passes and quality is >= RDG on the same sequences, commit to the NSS-derived architecture and start the data pipeline; if quality fails, widen to 64/128 channels at the 540p-and-below levels and retrain (budget allows it); if the HLSL port cannot match the golden tensors, the INT8 kernel path is the problem, not the model.
- Parallel on GPU 4: reproduce STSSNet on SunTemple to keep the FG option warm.
